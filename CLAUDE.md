# Creative direction for a new close-up star

A close-up is a **portrait**: one star, drawn so that someone who knows it would recognise it without the title, and could not mistake it for any of the others. How the knobs work is in `src/closeup/star.ts` and `docs/celestial/09-star-closeups.md`. This is what to aim them at.

## Find the signature

Every star gets a **signature**: the one or two real things an astronomer would say about it first. A spin that flattens it, a partner that feeds on it, a pulse, a wind, a ring, the nebula it lights, the planets it keeps. Decide the signature before touching the look, and build the whole portrait around it.

- **One idea leads.** A second may support it. A third is clutter: leave it to the caption.
- **Behaviour beats ornament.** If the star does something (beats, eclipses, blacks out, sheds shells), show it doing it. Motion is the strongest tool on the screen.
- **Company counts.** Partners, planets, cluster sisters and surrounding gas belong in the portrait when the star is known for them.
- **Calm is a look.** A quiet star stays quiet: Tau Ceti is spotless, Alkaid is plain on purpose. Restraint is their signature.

## Stay true, then turn it up

Everything on screen stands for something real about that star. For each element, answer "what real thing is this?", and remove the ones that are only there to look good.

The freedom is in **how strongly** a real thing is shown, so that it reads at a glance on a phone. A real feature may be drawn closer in, larger, brighter, faster or more regular than it really is. It may also stand in for something the eye could never see (ultraviolet light, patchy chemistry). It stays the same kind of thing.

Colour is physics: temperature picks the colour, hot stars are smooth, cool stars boil, and the bigger the star, the coarser the boiling. Bend a colour only when the legend is the story (Zubeneschamali's green).

Every such **liberty** is admitted, in plain words, in the caption.

## Make siblings, not copies

Before designing, look at the stars of the same kind that already exist and find what sets this one apart. Lean on real differences first: its companions, its surroundings, how we happen to see it. Where the truth leaves a choice open (an unknown orientation, where a far-off partner sits in the frame), spend that choice on variety.

## Picture and caption tell one story

The caption opens with the star's name and why it is worth a visit, and that reason is the signature. Whatever catches the eye on screen gets a beat saying what it is, and that beat admits the liberty taken in drawing it. If the picture shows something the caption never mentions, or the caption's main point is nowhere in the picture, one of them is wrong.

One exception: when the reason is a story or the star's place in the sky (Sualocin's joke name, Thuban the old pole star), no picture can show it. The caption leads with it, and the portrait stays a true likeness of the star.

## Write the caption

A caption is a short tour in beats, one idea each, in this order:

1. **Name and hook.** The first line starts with the star's name and says why it is worth a visit.
2. **The hook, deepened.** As many beats as the star earns on what makes it itself: what it does, how that was found out, what is on screen. Engaging and concise.
3. **The star beside the Sun.** Heat, size, brightness, age, distance and place in the sky, as comparisons a reader will keep: "twice as hot as the Sun", "in the Sun's place it would swallow Mars", "the light you see left in the 1400s". Round numbers, multiples of the Sun, distances in light-years.
4. **Stories.** A myth, a discovery, a use people made of the star: only where there is a story to tell. A name in another language is not one by itself. Tell each story at one star and point to it from the others.
5. **Similar stars.** The last beat names a few other stars in the app and what they share with this one.

When step 3 or 4 holds the hook (Polaris's place in the sky, Peacock's office-made name), it moves up to step 2 and is said once.

The fixed wordings for liberties, the sentence length that fits a phone page, and what the tests check are in `docs/celestial/09-star-closeups.md`.

## The standard

Judge the result on a phone, with the title covered:

- Would someone who knows this star name it?
- Does the signature read in the first two seconds?
- Beside its siblings, is it clearly itself?

Then tap through the caption on the same phone:

- Does the first page give a reason to read the second?
- Is everything that caught the eye explained?

Reference portraits: Achernar (flattened, ringed), Beta Lyrae (a teardrop feeding a disc), Betelgeuse (giant cells, a dust cloud), Fomalhaut (the off-centre ring), La Superba (soot over red), Alkaid (plain on purpose).

Reference captions: Sirius (the hook, then the hidden partner), Polaris (place in the sky first), Sualocin (the name is the story), Alkaid (calm, said plainly).
